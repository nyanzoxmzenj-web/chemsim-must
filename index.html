Experiment 1: Extraction of Cinnamaldehyde

Explore the isolation of cinnamaldehyde from cinnamon bark and investigate its aldehyde group and carbon-carbon unsaturation using simulated qualitative tests.

1. Aim and Theory

Aim: To extract cinnamaldehyde-containing oil from cinnamon bark and investigate functional groups in the extract.

Cinnamaldehyde is an aromatic aldehyde associated with cinnamon's characteristic smell. It contains an aldehyde group (–CHO) and a carbon-carbon double bond (C=C). Extraction separates components from plant material, but the extract may contain other compounds.

Learning outcomes

  • Explain why cinnamon is processed before extraction.
  • Identify evidence for an aldehyde group.
  • Interpret evidence for carbon-carbon unsaturation.
  • Recognise the limitations of qualitative tests.

Key terms

2. Apparatus and Materials

  • Cinnamon bark or powdered cinnamon
  • Approved extraction apparatus
  • Collection vessel and filter
  • Labelled test tubes and droppers
  • Brady's reagent and dilute Baeyer's reagent
  • Safety glasses, lab coat and suitable gloves

Use only the quantities, concentrations and methods specified in your approved laboratory manual.

3. Guided Procedure

Conceptual stages for learning. Follow your instructor's approved method in an actual laboratory.

1
Prepare the sample
Observe the cinnamon bark and prepare it as directed. Increasing surface area can help extraction.
2
Extract aromatic components
Use the approved extraction or steam-distillation setup under instructor supervision.
3
Collect the extract
Label the collected material and record its appearance. Do not directly inhale vapours.
4
Brady's test
A yellow/orange precipitate with 2,4-DNPH can indicate a carbonyl compound. It does not uniquely identify cinnamaldehyde.
5
Baeyer's test
Purple permanganate may fade and brown manganese dioxide may form when oxidisable unsaturation is present.
6
Record observations
Compare results with controls and interpret the limitations of each test.

9. Safety and Waste

  • Wear appropriate eye protection, lab coat and gloves.
  • Use Brady's reagent only under instructor supervision and dispose of it as hazardous waste.
  • Potassium permanganate is an oxidiser. Avoid contact and follow the approved disposal procedure.
  • Do not taste samples or directly inhale vapours.
  • Use heating equipment and solvents only as authorised.
